Overview

The course follows one question from start to finish: how does a machine turn light into an understanding of a scene? We begin with human vision and the digital image, work through classical image processing and hand-designed features, and then cover the deep networks used today for classification, detection and segmentation.

The final week turns to generative models, vision–language models such as CLIP, applications across domains, and the questions of robustness and fairness that come with deployment. Results in the lectures come from code run for the course on real photographs and public datasets.

Frequently asked questions

What do I need to know before the course?

Python, basic linear algebra (vectors, matrices, dot products), calculus up to the chain rule, and basic probability. Week 1 builds everything about images from scratch.

Do I need a GPU?

No. Every lecture example was run on an ordinary laptop CPU. For heavier tutorial work, use a free Google Colab or Kaggle Notebooks account.

Where do the labs happen?

Lectures are two hours each day. Tutorials and labs run separately with the tutors; that is where you work through the notebooks and get help with code.

How am I assessed?

A one-hour quiz each week, individual LaTeX-typed assignments in Weeks 1 and 2, and a team capstone project presented on the last day of Week 3.

How does the capstone work?

Teams of 2–3 choose one of twelve topics (or propose their own with a one-page synopsis) at the end of Week 2, build and evaluate a system in Week 3, and present it on the final day. See the capstone brief in Week 2.

Can I use AI tools?

Yes, as a tutor to help you understand, not as a ghost-writer. If you cannot explain your submission without the tool, it is not yet your work.

I'm stuck. Who do I ask?

Stuck on a bug for 30 minutes: ask a tutor or a classmate. Stuck on a concept for a day: ask the instructor after class or by email.
